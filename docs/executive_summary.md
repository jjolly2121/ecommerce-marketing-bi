# Executive Summary

The verified GA4 sample contains 360,129 sessions, 270,154 pseudonymous users, 5,279 governed orders, and $338,108 in recorded purchase revenue from 2020-11-01 through 2021-01-31. Overall session conversion was 1.35% and average order value was $64.05.

The clearest behavioral opportunity is retention and return traffic. Returning sessions represented 27.5% of traffic but 66.9% of revenue and converted 4.553 times as often as new sessions. The approximate 95% interval for the conversion difference was +2.308 to +2.534 percentage points, subject to repeated-session dependence and non-causal interpretation. In the ordered same-session funnel, 19.69% of product-view sessions reached cart, 35.71% of those sessions then reached checkout, and 52.33% then purchased. Separately, 43.63% of all checkout sessions contained a purchase, regardless of earlier path.

Channel results suggest Referral traffic deserves closer investigation: it produced a 1.66% conversion rate and $1.24 revenue per session, compared with 0.98% and $0.54 for Paid Search. These figures do not support a budget-allocation claim because the sample contains no media cost and uses first-user acquisition fields.

The strongest measurement warning is late-January revenue completeness. In the week of 2021-01-25, 218 of 321 governed orders have zero recorded revenue. The apparent 80.6% week-over-week revenue decline should not be treated as a business-performance conclusion until transaction values are reconciled.

Recommended next actions are to validate and test product-view-to-cart friction, run a measured second-purchase lifecycle test, and repair late-January purchase-value instrumentation before using the final week for revenue or channel decisions.
